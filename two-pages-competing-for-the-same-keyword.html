Two pages competing for the same keyword?

If two of your own pages try to rank for the same search, Google has to guess which one to show, and often shows neither of them high up.

In plain words

What is going on

This is called keyword cannibalization. It happens when a blog post and a service page, or two similar blog posts, both cover the same topic. Google keeps switching between them, splits the links and clicks, and neither page builds enough strength.

It is common on sites that have published for a few years, and it is invisible unless you look at Search Console data per search query.

How to tell

Signs you have this problem

  • In Search Console, one search query shows clicks to two or more of your pages.
  • The page Google shows for a query keeps changing from week to week.
  • A blog post ranks for your main service keyword instead of your service page.
  • You have several posts on almost the same topic (for example "teeth whitening cost" and "how much is teeth whitening").

Ticked two or more? Ask for a free audit and we check it properly on your live pages.

Why it matters

Why it costs you money

01

The page meant to convert (your service or product page) loses out to a page that doesn't sell.

02

Splitting links and clicks across two pages keeps both lower than one strong page would be.

03

Writing more content on the same topic makes the problem worse, not better.

In Kuwait and Saudi Arabia

Bilingual sites in the Gulf can also compete with themselves when the Arabic and English pages are not marked as translations of each other, or when both languages are mixed on one page.

FAQ

Questions about this problem

Is cannibalization always bad?

No. If two pages answer different needs and both rank well, leave them. It is a problem when it holds your most important page back.

Do I need to delete pages?

Rarely. Usually the weaker page is merged into the stronger one and redirected, so nothing is lost.

What data do you need?

Read access to Google Search Console. We never need your password; you add us as a user.

How do you know a fix worked?

We record the positions and clicks for the affected queries before the change and compare the same queries afterwards.
