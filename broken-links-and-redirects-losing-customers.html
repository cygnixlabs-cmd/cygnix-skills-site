In plain words

What is going on

A redirect sends people (and Google) from an old address to a new one. When a page is moved or deleted without a proper redirect, links to it break and show a 404 error. When redirects point to other redirects, they form chains or loops that slow pages down or fail completely.

Other websites that once linked to your old pages (a newspaper, a directory, a partner) are valuable. If those pages now return 404, you lose the value of those links. Reclaiming them is often one of the quickest wins in SEO.

How to tell

Signs you have this problem

  • You changed your website, platform or URL structure in the last two years.
  • Old links in emails, social posts or Google results land on "Page not found".
  • Old product or service pages redirect to the homepage instead of the matching new page.
  • Search Console shows a growing list of 404 or "Page with redirect" errors.
  • Other sites still link to pages on your site that no longer exist.

FAQ

Questions about this problem

What is the difference between a 301 and a 302 redirect?

A 301 tells Google the move is permanent and passes the old page's value to the new one. A 302 says it is temporary. For a page that has moved for good, use a 301.

Is redirecting everything to the homepage OK?

No. Google often treats that like a broken page. Each old URL should go to the closest matching new page.

What is backlink reclamation?

Finding links from other websites that point to pages on your site that now return an error, and redirecting those old addresses to a live page so you get the value back.

Can you fix redirects without access to my site?

We can produce the full redirect map without access. To apply it we need an editor role or your developer's help. No passwords.
